## Topic Overview

Large Language Models (LLMs) have become powerful tools for generating and processing natural language and are increasingly being used in research applications such as literature review, scientific writing, question answering, summarization, information retrieval, and research assistance. Despite their capabilities, LLMs can generate information that is inaccurate, unsupported, misleading, or inconsistent with reliable sources. These errors are commonly referred to as **hallucinations**.

Hallucinations are particularly concerning in research environments because fabricated facts, incorrect explanations, nonexistent references, and inaccurate citations can affect the reliability of academic and scientific work. Understanding the different forms of hallucination is therefore important for identifying their causes, measuring their occurrence, and developing appropriate methods to reduce them.

Research in this area includes the classification and taxonomy of hallucination types, automatic hallucination detection, factuality evaluation, citation verification, source grounding, retrieval-augmented generation, and hallucination mitigation. Different approaches examine whether generated content is supported by evidence, consistent with a given context, factually correct, or reliable for a particular application.

This repository organizes research resources related to **LLM hallucinations for research applications**. It provides a curated collection of scholarly papers, datasets, software tools, GitHub implementations, and learning resources to support the study of hallucination types, detection, evaluation, and mitigation.
